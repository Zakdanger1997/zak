Notes & Safety

  • Reversing = phase swap. The DOWN (KM2) and ← (KM4) contactors swap two of the three phases (L1↔L3) relative to UP/→. Confirm actual direction with a no-load rotation test and relabel if needed — do not assume.
  • Interlock both ways. Use contactor pairs that are mechanically interlocked and keep the electrical interlock shown (opposite-direction NC contact in each coil rung) so UP and DOWN can never close together.
  • Fail-safe brake. The brake is spring-applied and electrically released. Any loss of power, E-stop, or overload applies the brake and holds the load. Never wire a brake that releases on power loss.
  • Dead-man control. Pendant buttons are momentary — motion only while held. The hoist should have no mechanical latching.
  • Limit switches are NC and wired in series with their direction, so a broken wire stops motion (fail-safe).
  • Earthing. Bond motor frames, panel, pendant and crane structure to PE. Verify continuity before energising.
  • Sizing. Set overloads to the motor nameplate FLA; size cable, contactors and fuses to the motors' full-load and starting currents.
Important: This is a standard reference schematic to assemble and understand the panel — it is not a substitute for a qualified electrical engineer's approved design. Crane/lifting installations are safety-critical and are usually subject to local regulations and standards (e.g. IEC 60204-32 for machinery hoisting, plus local wiring codes). Have the final design checked and commissioned by a competent person, and confirm every rating against the actual motor and brake nameplates.
